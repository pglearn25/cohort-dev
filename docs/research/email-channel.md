# Research: email as a notification channel for Cadence (issue #4)

Date: 2026-11-11. Sources are primary (Google, Yahoo, FTC, ICO, RFC Editor, vendor docs), fetched on this date. Claims not backed by a source are labelled "Guess:". The hosted vendor is not chosen yet (other map tickets), so Novu is used only as a worked example of a headless vendor.

## 1. Sending domain setup (SPF / DKIM / DMARC)

- All senders to Gmail must set up SPF or DKIM, have valid forward and reverse DNS (PTR), use TLS, follow RFC 5322, and keep the spam rate below 0.3% (Google, [Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Google recommends staying under 0.10%.
- "Bulk senders" (more than 5,000 messages/day to Gmail) must set up SPF **and** DKIM **and** DMARC (policy may be `none`), and the From domain must align with the SPF or DKIM domain (same Google page). DKIM keys must be 1024 bits or longer; 2048 recommended.
- Yahoo: SPF or DKIM minimum for all senders; both plus a DMARC policy of at least `p=none` for bulk senders; spam rate below 0.3% (Yahoo, [Sender best practices](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)). Yahoo's page does not define the bulk threshold.
- Cost to Cadence: a few DNS records (SPF, DKIM CNAME/TXT, DMARC TXT) on a domain we control, normally generated by the email provider. Guess: under an hour of work plus ongoing monitoring of DMARC reports and Postmaster Tools.
- Guess: Cadence is unlikely to reach 5,000 emails/day to Gmail early on, but setting up all three is cheap and mailbox providers apply the "all senders" rules regardless.

## 2. Deliverability

- Main levers stated by providers: authentication, a spam-complaint rate under 0.3% (target under 0.1%), TLS, valid PTR (Google and Yahoo pages above).
- Transactional-style mail (refund decided, seat claimed) is triggered by a user action and expected, which keeps complaint rates low. Guess: sending it from a separate subdomain from any future marketing mail protects its reputation; not verified against a primary source.
- Google and Yahoo do not publish inbox-placement guarantees; no primary source promises deliverability.

## 3. Unsubscribe and consent

- **US, CAN-SPAM** ([FTC compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)): "transactional or relationship" messages (agreed transactions, account/membership change notices, warranty/safety info, etc.) are exempt from most provisions but must not have false or misleading routing information. The categories are read narrowly. Commercial messages need an opt-out honoured within 10 business days, a working opt-out for 30 days after sending, and a valid physical postal address. Penalty up to $53,088 per violating email. Mixed messages are treated as commercial if the subject suggests an ad or transactional content is not mainly at the start.
  - Implication: "refund decided" and "seat claimed" are likely transactional; "new course you might like" is commercial. Guess: Cadence's notification list will mostly be transactional, but that is a legal classification we have not had reviewed.
- **Google/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe** applies to "marketing messages and subscribed messages" from bulk senders; Google's page does not address transactional mail either way. Yahoo requires honouring unsubscribes within 2 days. Mechanism is RFC 8058: `List-Unsubscribe` with one HTTPS URI, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, DKIM-signed, no redirect, no cookies ([RFC 8058](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8058)).
- **UK PECR** ([ICO, electronic mail marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/)): marketing email needs specific consent unless the "soft opt-in" for existing customers applies, with an opt-out in every message. The fetched page covers marketing only; it does not define service messages.
- **EU GDPR**: not verified in this research (no regulator page was fetched). Guess: service messages needed to perform the contract do not need marketing consent, but a regulator source should be read before relying on this.
- Net: a notification email feature needs an unsubscribe/preference path for any non-transactional category, and a documented split between transactional and optional categories. This feeds the preferences question left open on the map.

## 4. Does the vendor send email, or is a separate provider needed?

- Novu (example headless vendor): lists email as a channel on all plans, but its docs say it connects to providers (SendGrid, Amazon SES, Resend, etc.) and you "must set up each provider individually in the Novu dashboard". A demo email integration exists for testing only; its limits were not stated on the page fetched ([Novu email integrations](https://docs.novu.co/platform/integrations/email)).
- So with a vendor like this, **email means one extra signup (an email provider) plus the DNS setup above**, unless the chosen vendor sends email natively. Guess: other vendors (Knock, Courier, etc.) behave similarly; their docs were not checked. Verify on the shortlisted vendor's own docs before deciding.

## 5. Cost

- Novu free plan: 10,000 workflow runs/month, 5 workflows, 2 environments (dev + prod), 3 members, 24h activity retention; all channels included. Pro from $30/month for 30,000 runs ([Novu pricing](https://novu.co/pricing)). Email does not add a Novu charge on the page, but the provider is billed separately.
- Amazon SES: $0.10 per 1,000 emails à la carte; no permanent free tier, only new-account credits up to $200 for 12 months ([SES pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/ses/pricing/)). Guess: at Cadence's likely volume the provider fee is negligible; the real cost is setup and compliance.
- Other providers' free tiers were not checked.

## Conclusion: what adopting email costs Cadence

1. One more service signup (an email provider), unless the chosen vendor sends natively (not the case for the Novu example).
2. DNS work on a sending domain: SPF, DKIM, DMARC; PTR handled by the provider.
3. Compliance work: classify each Notification as transactional or optional; unsubscribe link and one-click headers (RFC 8058) for optional categories; physical address on commercial mail; honour opt-outs within 2 days (Yahoo) / 10 business days (CAN-SPAM).
4. Ongoing: monitor complaint rate (<0.3%), bounces, DMARC reports.
5. Money: small (provider about $0.10 per 1,000 emails; vendor free tier likely enough for development).

Guess (not a verified decision): to minimise signups, email is the one channel that forces a second service. Treat email as optional for v1 and limited to a few transactional events (refund decided, seat claimed), or defer it. The decision belongs to the downstream ticket (#6).
